📖 What Is This?

The CGNR Matrix is a mathematical framework for computing evidence-weighted nutritional intervention scores for patients recovering from total or subtotal gastrectomy, typically performed for gastric or esophageal cancer. It uses a 3-mode tensor model to combine clinical evidence, hormonal state, and patient compliance into a single decision vector of 9 intervention scores.

The core problem: After gastrectomy, patients enter a catabolic crisis — severe inflammation drives hormonal disruption that can inflate nutritional evidence scores up to 60×, producing dangerously aggressive recommendations. The CGNR Matrix intercepts this through three deterministic safety gates before any score reaches the clinician.

This is a research prototype, not a medical device or FDA-cleared software. The hormonal-attenuation constants are theoretical baselines. Do not use this for actual clinical decisions without clinical calibration and regulatory review.

🏥 Clinical Use Case

Post-Gastrectomy Oncology Nutrition

Gastrectomy (removal of the stomach, partial or total) is the primary curative treatment for gastric cancer. Post-surgery, patients face:

  • Severe catabolic state — the body breaks down muscle and fat at an accelerated rate, driven by surgical trauma and systemic inflammation
  • Hormonal disruption — ghrelin (hunger hormone) collapses after stomach removal; leptin, insulin, and GLP-1 signalling are disrupted
  • CRP elevation — C-reactive protein rises as a systemic inflammation marker. CRP > 50 mg/dL signals potential sepsis, where standard nutritional logic becomes invalid
  • CORCONDIA diagnostic — core consistency of the tensor decomposition; a measure of whether the statistical model is sound or has degraded (rank erasure)
  • Patient compliance variability — patients may refuse specific interventions (e.g., a vegan patient refusing MCT oil, or a patient with dumping syndrome refusing high-osmolarity feeds)

The CGNR Matrix computes a 9-dimensional evidence vector, one score per intervention, and applies three safety gates to ensure the output is statistically sound, hormonally calibrated, and respects patient autonomy.

⚙️ How the Pipeline Works
Three deterministic gates, executed sequentially. Any gate can block the entire pipeline with a Hard Stop.
GATE 1

Dynamic Floor

Resolves SPOF P0-1: Sepsis Score Inflation

Computes the hormonal attenuation coefficient h(CRP) from the patient's C-reactive protein level. At low CRP (no inflammation), h ≈ 1.0 (full hormonal contribution). As CRP rises toward the sepsis threshold (50 mg/dL), h collapses toward 0. At CRP ≥ 50, the gate issues a Hard Stop — the hormonal signal is set to exactly 0.

h = 1 / (1 + e^(k·(CRP − 25)))

Without this gate, hormonal factors inflate evidence scores up to 60× during catabolic crisis, producing dangerously aggressive recommendations.

GATE 2

3σ Residual Norm

Resolves SPOF P0-2: Rank Erasure

Checks the CORCONDIA core consistency — a diagnostic that measures whether the tensor decomposition is statistically sound. If CORCONDIA falls below μ − 3σ = 70% (target 85%, σ=5%), the model has suffered rank erasure and its outputs are corrupt.

Accept if CORCONDIA ≥ 70

This is a one-sided check — a higher CORCONDIA (better fit) never fails. The original implementation flagged perfect fits as corrupt due to a symmetric abs() error.

GATE 3

Zero-Veto Compliance

Resolves SPOF P0-3: Patient Abandonment

Scales each evidence score by the patient's compliance factor f ∈ [0,1]⁹. A refused intervention (f=0) zeros only that score — the other 8 proceed. If ≥4 of 9 interventions are refused, the protocol is non-viable (Tier 3 escalation).

E_gated = E(B) × f (element-wise)

The original design used a scalar compliance factor — one refusal zeroed the entire protocol, abandoning the patient entirely.

Post-Gate Safeguards

After the three gates, two numerical safeguards protect the geometric-mean scoring pipeline:

  • CLIP[0,1] (S-22): All scores are clamped to [0, 1] to prevent range violations
  • Laplace zero-guard (S-06): Exact zeros are replaced with ε=1e-6 to prevent log(0) crashes in the geometric mean, then re-clipped to [0, 1]

📊 Pipeline Result

⚠️ Edge Cases & Safety Behavior
🔴 Gate 1: Sepsis Hard Stop (CRP ≥ 50 mg/dL) ▾

When CRP reaches or exceeds 50 mg/dL, the hormonal attenuation coefficient h is set to exactly 0.0 and the pipeline blocks immediately.

Clinical meaning: At sepsis-level inflammation, the hormonal disruption is so severe that nutritional evidence scores are unreliable. Any recommendation would be based on corrupted hormonal input. The system refuses to produce a recommendation — the clinician must treat the sepsis first, then re-evaluate.

What the clinician sees: A RED status with GATE_1: Sepsis detected (CRP 55.0 ≥ 50.0). No scores are produced.

🔴 Gate 2: Rank Erasure (CORCONDIA < 70%) ▾

When CORCONDIA falls below 70% (μ − 3σ = 85 − 15), the tensor decomposition has suffered rank erasure — the statistical model is no longer sound.

Clinical meaning: The evidence scores E(B) are derived from a tensor decomposition. If the decomposition degrades (too few data points, collinear factors, numerical instability), the scores are statistically corrupt but may look valid. Gate 2 catches this before it reaches the clinician.

Why one-sided: CORCONDIA is a "higher is better" metric. A value of 100% (perfect fit) is better, not suspicious. The check only blocks when the value is too low.

What the clinician sees: A RED status with GATE_2: CORCONDIA 45.00 below 3σ floor 70.00. No scores are produced.

🔴 Gate 3: Tier 3 Escalation (≥4/9 refusals) ▾

When 4 or more of the 9 interventions are refused (f=0), the protocol is deemed non-viable and the pipeline blocks.

Clinical meaning: With fewer than 6 viable interventions, the nutritional protocol cannot provide adequate coverage. This triggers Tier 3 escalation — the case is escalated to the Multidisciplinary Team (MDT) for an alternative care plan.

Why per-intervention, not scalar: The original design used a single scalar compliance factor. One refusal (f=0) zeroed the entire protocol — the patient was abandoned nutritionally. The vector approach (f ∈ [0,1]⁹) allows each intervention to be independently vetoed.

What the clinician sees: A RED status with GATE_3: Protocol non-viable (5/9 interventions refused). No scores are produced.

🛡️ Laplace Zero-Guard & CLIP Invariant ▾

After gating, if an intervention was refused (f=0), its gated score becomes exactly 0.0. The geometric-mean scoring pipeline would crash on log(0).

S-06 (Laplace): Replaces exact zeros with ε = 1e-6 — a negligible value that prevents the crash without materially affecting the score.

S-22 (CLIP): All scores are clamped to [0, 1] before and after the Laplace step, preserving the range invariant. The re-clip after Laplace is critical — the original implementation added ε to all elements, pushing 1.0 → 1.000001 and violating the invariant.

What the clinician sees: A refused intervention shows a score of 0.000001 (effectively 0) in the output vector, while all other scores remain correctly in [0, 1].

🔬 NaN/Inf Rejection ▾

All input paths reject NaN (Not a Number) and Inf (Infinity) with a ValueError before any gate logic executes.

Why this matters: In a clinical setting, a sensor malfunction or data pipeline error can produce NaN. If NaN propagates through the gates, it silently produces a NaN score that might appear as a valid number in the clinician's dashboard. The CGNR Matrix rejects any non-finite input immediately.

What the clinician sees: An explicit error — ValueError: crp_value must be finite (got nan) — rather than a silent NaN in the output.

👨‍⚕️ How to Use This Clinically
  1. Obtain the patient's CRP from the latest blood panel. Enter it in mg/dL.
  2. Obtain the CORCONDIA value from the tensor decomposition run (this is computed by the solver layer, not manually).
  3. Review the evidence scores E(B) — these are the raw priors from the tensor model, one per intervention.
  4. Assess patient compliance for each of the 9 interventions. Set f=1 for full compliance, f=0.5 for partial, f=0 for refused.
  5. Click Evaluate. The pipeline runs all three gates sequentially.
  6. Interpret the result:
    • 🟢 GREEN: All gates passed. The 9 scores are your evidence-weighted, compliance-adjusted recommendations. Higher score = stronger evidence for that intervention.
    • 🔴 RED: A gate blocked the pipeline. Read the incident to understand why. Do not proceed with the raw E(B) scores — they are ungated and potentially unsafe.
  7. For RED results: Treat the underlying condition first (sepsis, rank corruption, or compliance issue), then re-evaluate. For Tier 3 escalation, convene the MDT.

📐 Mathematical Reference
Gate 1 — Hormonal attenuation coefficient:
h(CRP) = 1 / (1 + exp(k · (CRP − midpoint)))

k = 0.18, midpoint = 25 mg/dL, threshold = 50 mg/dL. Hard Stop if CRP ≥ threshold (h = 0).

Gate 2 — One-sided 3σ lower bound:
Accept if CORCONDIA ≥ μ − 3σ

μ = 85%, σ = 5%, floor = 70%. RankFailure if CORCONDIA < floor.

Gate 3 — Element-wise compliance scaling:
E_gated[i] = E(B)[i] × f[i], ∀i ∈ {1..9}

Hard Stop if count(f[i] ≤ 0) ≥ 4 (Tier 3 threshold).

Safeguards:
v = CLIP(E_gated, 0, 1) → WHERE(v==0, 1e-6, v) → CLIP(v, 0, 1)

S-22 (CLIP) + S-06 (Laplace ε = 1e-6). Re-clip preserves [0,1] invariant.
